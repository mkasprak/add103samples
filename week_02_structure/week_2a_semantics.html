Overview

Last week, we wrote a basic "Hello World" page. But professional websites aren't just random text. They have a structure.

Today, we learn Semantic HTML: using tags that describe what the content is, not just how it looks.

✨ Why this matters

Google relies on semantic tags to know what your page is about (SEO). More importantly, screen readers rely on them to navigate. If you use generic tags for everything, you are building a digital maze that excludes users with disabilities.

📖 The Textbook: Building Structure

Click the sections below to read about the "Big 4" Semantic Tags.

1. The Header (<header>)

Definition: The introductory content for a page or section.

Think of the header as the "Welcome Mat" of your website. It typically contains:

  • The Logo or Site Name
  • Main Navigation Links
  • A Search Bar
2. The Main Content (<main>)

Definition: The dominant content of the document.

This is unique to the page. You should only have one <main> tag per page.

3. The Article (<article>) vs Section (<section>)

This is where it gets tricky:

  • <article>: Independent content. If you took it out of the page and put it in a newspaper, it would still make sense (e.g., a blog post, a news story).
  • <section>: A thematic grouping of content. Usually has a heading.
4. The Footer (<footer>)

Definition: Content at the bottom of the document.

Footers are the "safety net" of a webpage. Contains Copyright info, Contact details, social links.

👀 Look & Learn: The Article

This is how we structure a blog post or news story.

🔗 View Live Sample: The Birdwatcher Log

Study the code below. This is the Standard Layout used by 99% of websites.

<!-- =========================================
     EXAMPLE: The 'Holy Grail' Structure
     ========================================= -->
<body>

    <!-- 1. TOP SECTION -->
    <header>
        <h1>The Daily Tech News</h1>
        <nav>
            <a href="index.html">Home</a> | <a href="contact.html">Contact</a>
        </nav>
    </header>

    <!-- 2. CONTENT SECTION (Unique Stuff) -->
    <main>
        
        <!-- Primary Article -->
        <article>
            <h2>New iPhone Released</h2>
            <p>Today Apple announced...</p>
        </article>

        <!-- Another Article -->
        <article>
            <h2>Web Design Trends 2026</h2>
            <p>Inline styles are back in fashion...</p>
        </article>

    </main>

    <!-- 3. BOTTOM SECTION -->
    <footer>
        <p>© 2026 Meri Kasprak</p>
    </footer>

</body>
Observation:
  • Notice how header, main, and footer are siblings? They don't go inside each other.
  • The nav tag is nested inside the header. This is common practice.

💻 Project: The Semantic Biography

You are updated your "Hello World" page into a structured Bio Page.

📋 Directions

  1. Open your ADD103/week_01/index.html (or create `week_02/bio.html`).
  2. Delete the old content inside <body>.
  3. Header: Create a <header> that contains an <h1> with your full name and a <nav> with 3 links (dummy links like `#` are fine).
  4. Main: Create a <main> section. Inside it, create two separate <section> blocks:
    • Section 1: "About Me" (Intro Paragraph)
    • Section 2: "My Hobbies" (A list of 3 things you love)
  5. Footer: Create a <footer> that says "© 2026 [Your Name] | Contact me at [Email]".
Self-Check: Right-click and "Inspect" in Chrome. Collapse the header, main, and footer tags. Do they fold up neatly? If yes, your hierarchy is perfect.
